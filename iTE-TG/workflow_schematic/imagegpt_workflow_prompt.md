# iTE/TG workflow schematic prompt

Create a Nature Machine Intelligence-style scientific workflow schematic for
a manuscript about forecasting research directions in ionic thermoelectrics
(iTE) and thermogalvanic systems (TG).

## Format and style

- Wide 16:9 landscape.
- Clean white background.
- Flat vector-like editorial scientific illustration.
- Crisp geometry and generous spacing.
- Restrained charcoal, muted teal, warm red, pale gold, and light-grey palette.
- No purple gradients, decorative blobs, logos, journal branding, or fabricated values.

## Composition

Show a clear left-to-right pipeline with five stages connected by thin
directional arrows.

### 1. Literature corpus

Show two compact stacks of paper abstracts, one labelled exactly `iTE` and one
labelled exactly `TG`, arranged along a publication-year timeline.

### 2. LLM concept mining

Show abstract text entering a language-model/token motif and producing four
concept categories labelled exactly:

- `Materials`
- `Mechanisms`
- `Structures`
- `Functions`

Show duplicate raw phrases merging into a cleaned canonical ontology.

### 3. Representation

Show two complementary representations:

- a semantic 2D point cloud labelled `Embedding`;
- a year-layered concept network labelled `Temporal graph`.

### 4. Temporal prediction

Make this the visual focal stage. Split it into three clean lanes labelled
exactly:

- `iTE → iTE`
- `iTE → TG`
- `TG → TG`

Each lane should show historical concepts at time `t`, candidate concept pairs,
and a future window `t+1`. Clearly distinguish internal evolution from
cross-domain knowledge transfer. Include only the short method labels:

- `ML`
- `Graph`
- `Hybrid`

### 5. Scientific insight

Recombine the three branches into two compact matrix icons labelled exactly:

- `Material × Mechanism`
- `Mechanism × Mechanism`

Then point to three experimentally actionable motifs:

- dynamic gel network;
- porous electrode interface;
- ion/solvation transport.

End with the short label `Research directions`.

## Scientific constraints

- Depict co-occurrence and knowledge-flow forecasting conceptually, not as causal proof.
- Do not include numerical metrics, performance curves, confusion matrices, microscopy, people, laboratory equipment, institutional marks, or unsupported mechanisms.
- Do not draw detailed chemical structures.
- Keep all text sparse, horizontal, correctly spelled, and highly legible.
- Treat the result as a conceptual workflow draft rather than a quantitative evidence panel.

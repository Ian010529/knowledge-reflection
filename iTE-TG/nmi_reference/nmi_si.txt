 2   Evaluating LLM-based Concept Extraction
 3   To construct a held-out test set, three annotators (n=3) manually annotated five abstracts that
 4   were not part of the training data. The resulting inter-annotator agreement, measured as the mean
 5   pairwise agreement, was 0.345 (pairwise scores: 0.215, 0.326, 0.493; all values rounded to three
 6   decimal places).
 7       Importantly, none of the human-annotated concepts were incorrect. Disagreement primarily
 8   stemmed from varying emphasis on annotation breadth versus depth. As all extracted concepts
 9   were valid, we defined the union of all annotations as the ground truth for evaluating our LLM-
10   based concept extraction approach against established methods, namely KeyBERT, RAKE, and
11   SciSpacy.
12       RAKE achieved slightly higher recall than the LLM-based approach, primarily due to its exten-
13   sive extraction of candidate terms. However, this also resulted in substantially lower precision, as
14   for every relevant concept, approximately five irrelevant ones were produced (precision ≈ 16.7%).
15   When balancing precision and recall, the LLM-based method outperformed all other approaches,
16   achieving a substantially higher precision at similar recall compared to RAKE.
17       Notably, concepts produced by the LLM that were not counted as true positives tended to
18   be semantically relevant but either overly broad or overly specific (e.g., ‘aerospace part thickness
19   measurement’), whereas competing methods frequently generated entirely irrelevant outputs such
20   as ‘6148 10 3 cycles’, ‘increase’, ‘interdisciplinary field involving principles’, or ‘317’. This highlights
21   the practical advantage of the LLM-based approach: it substantially reduces the need for manual
22   post-processing, as the extracted concepts are largely free of irrelevant entries.
23       To enable researchers to benchmark their own models, all labeled data can be found on our
24   GitHub repository.

Strengthening mechanisms in short carbon fiber reinforced Nb/Nb5Si3 composites with interfacial reac-
tion. The understanding of strengthening mechanisms in composites is crucial to improve its mechanical
properties. Strengthening mechanisms in Nb/Nb5Si3 composites reinforced by different contents of short
carbon fiber C sf were investigated. Interface and tensile properties of the composites were studied.
Interfacial nano-sized Nb3 phase forms by in situ interfacial reaction, the Nb3 phase embedded intimately
in the C sf and matrix forms an anchor effect. The content of Nb3 phases in the composites increases
because of the increase of C sf content. Ultimate tensile strength and yield strength of the composite are
improved because of the increase of C sf content. When the content of C sf is 1.0 wt%, the elongation
reaches the maximum, but the elongation decreases slightly when the content of C sf increases from 1.0
wt % to 2.0 wt %. The strengthening mechanisms in this composite were investigated according to grain
refinement and load transfer. Load transfer is main strengthening mechanism and accounts for more than
95% of the improvement in yield strength. This indicates that the control of interfacial bonding state
is crucial to improve the mechanical properties of the composites. This study can offer some valuable
information to disclosing the strengthening behaviors in the C sf reinforced metal matrix composites.

Abstract
Crystallization behaviour of AlSm amorphous alloys. Various Al100xSmx alloys 10 14 have been rapidly
solidified by single-roller melt spinning with careful control of the atmosphere in the quenching device. The
structural state and subsequent devitrification behaviour of the melt-spun ribbons are found to be partic-
ularly sensitive to the quenching conditions. Except for the thinnest ribbons, there are inhomogeneities
both through the ribbon thickness and along the length. Both fully and partially amorphous ribbons
have been obtained. The crystallization processes of the amorphous phases have been followed by X-ray
diffraction, transmission electron microscopy and differential scanning calorimetry DSC. Al92Sm8 shows
primary crystallization of Al, followed by the formation of metastable phases; Al90Sm10 transforms
polymorphically to a metastable intermetallic; Al88Sm12 and Al86Sm14 display eutectic crystallization
into Al and a metastable mixture of compounds. For Al90Sm10, the DSC traces involve several overlapping
peaks. This may be the result of transformations occurring in distinct parts of the ribbon with different
mechanisms. A kinetic analysis of the crystallization processes has been performed by means of isothermal
and non-isothermal DSC experiments. A discussion of the kinetic parameters derived from Kissinger and
Avrami analyses is provided.

28   Test set construction
29   To get a clear picture of our models’ performances, we do not perform oversampling for the positives
30   during test set construction. This is necessary to analyze AUROC, precision and recall in a setting
31   as close to real-world applications as possible. We approach test set construction by randomly
32   sampling node pairs that are not connected until the end of 2019 and simply storing the label by
33   looking three years ahead in the graph. The resulting 2,000,000 node pairs, therefore, mirror the
34   real distribution of positives. Of these 307 (0.015%) positive examples, 87 links were formed in the
35   year 2020, 123 in 2021, and 97 in 2022.

36   Evaluation against Science4Cast
37   We benchmark our Baseline model against the Science4Cast benchmark described in [14]. First,
38   we train the model on data up until 2014 to predict links emerging in 2015, 2016 and 2017. Second,
39   we evaluate on the node pairs contained in ‘SemanticGraph_delta_3_cutoff_0_minedge_1.pkl’.
40   Surprisingly, our Baseline model is not only competitive but would have scored the second place
41   in the Science4Cast competition with an AUROC of 0.9088. Supplementary Table 3 presents an
42   exhaustive overview of precision, recall, F1 score and the raw data at different thresholds.

43   Analysis of Ensemble Performance Across Feature Domains
44   We investigated the impact of model blending on AUC performance, i.e. whether performance gains
45   arise from combining distinct feature modalities or solely from the averaging effect of ensembling.
46   While blends of models with complementary information (embeddings mixed with topological
47   models, Panels 1 and 2 of Supplementary Fig. 10) achieve substantial performance boosts, blends
48   within the same feature domain (Panel 3) or model type (Panel 4) conversely result in only marginal
49   improvements. This demonstrates that the observed gains are driven by the synergy of non-
50   redundant features rather than simple ensemble averaging.

Nanoporous Structure and High Entropy Alloy

Nanoporous structures combined with high entropy alloys ( HEAs )
   represent an intriguing area of study due to the unique
   properties HEAs offer , such as exceptional mechanical strength ,
   corrosion resistance , and thermal stability . The introduction of
    a nanoporous architecture can potentially enhance these
   properties by introducing lightweight characteristics and
   increasing surface area , which could be particularly beneficial
   for applications in catalysis and energy storage . Combining
   these two concepts could lead to the development of new ,
   optimized materials with tailored properties for specific
   industrial applications .

In Situ High Resolution Transmission Electron Microscopy and
   Mechanical Performance

The application of in situ high - resolution transmission electron
   microscopy ( HRTEM ) to study the mechanical performance of
   materials offers a promising research direction . This technique
   allows real - time imaging of material deformation , phase
   transformations , and dislocation dynamics at the atomic scale
   during mechanical testing . Understanding these microscopic
   events in great detail will enable the design of materials with
   superior mechanical properties tailored to specific applications
   , such clarification provides deeper insights into materials
   behavior under stress and can lead to the development of
   materials with enhanced durability and performance .

Epitaxial Strain and Ultraviolet Visible Spectroscopy

Studying the effects of epitaxial strain using ultraviolet - visible
   spectroscopy could open new pathways in the synthesis and
   characterization of thin films and nanostructures . Epitaxial
   strain has been known to significantly alter the electronic and
   optical properties of materials . By applying UV - Vis spectroscopy
   , researchers can gain valuable insights into the changes in
   band structure and electronic transitions caused by strain . This
    combination could be particularly valuable in the development
   of advanced optical devices , including lasers , LEDs , and
   photovoltaic cells , where precise control over electronic
   properties is crucial .

116       Below is a more detailed discussion of the 5 selected examples of new combinations of concepts
117   shown in the results section of the main text.

118   Suggestion: "Conventional ceramic" + "Graphene oxide"
119   During the concept prediction phase of the AI model trained on open-access scientific abstracts, an
120   interesting connection between two research areas emerged. The AI identified “conventional ceram-
121   ics” and “graphene oxide” as a potentially promising combination. While the integration of graphene
122   oxide with ceramics has been explored in numerous studies, these approaches typically rely on dif-
123   ferent synthesis methods and subsequent mixing. In contrast, the method presented here is both
124   unique and simple, yielding a distinctive material: a nanometer-thin multilayer graphene scaffold
125   coated with an approximately 200 nm thick iron oxide layer (see Extended Data Fig. 2??). This
126   AI-predicted research direction aligned with prior experimental work that had been conducted but
127   never published, highlighting the model’s potential to identify overlooked but valuable material
128   synthesis strategies.
129       At first glance, the connection between conventional ceramics and graphene oxide is not obvious
130   due to their fundamentally different structures and chemical behaviors. Conventional ceramics are
131   typically ionic solids, composed of various cation-anion combinations (e.g., transition metals as
132   cations and oxides, fluorides, or sulfides as anions). They are known for their rigidity, thermal and
133   chemical stability, and widespread use in electronic and electrochemical applications. In contrast,
134   graphene oxide consists of covalently bonded carbon and oxygen atoms, forming a highly flexible
135   material that exfoliates and decomposes at elevated temperatures, producing carbon oxide species
136   and exfoliated (multilayer)graphene.
137       The link between these material classes becomes evident when they are combined into a com-
138   posite system. However, this integration is challenging due to their distinct synthesis methods
139   and reactivity. In previous experiments aimed at developing a high-conductivity electrode mate-
140   rial for conversion type Li-ion batteries, a successful combination of these materials was achieved.
141   Graphene oxide was mixed with iron pentacarbonyl (Fe(CO)5 ), a liquid at ambient conditions, and
142   subjected to autoclave treatment at temperatures above 250 ◦ C. Under these conditions, Fe(CO)5
143   vaporized and decomposed into atomic Fe(0) species in the gas phase at around 200 ◦ C. These iron
144   atoms subsequently reacted with the oxygen groups in graphene oxide, forming iron oxide species
145   (Fe2 O3 , Fe3 O4 ) while simultaneously reducing graphene oxide to graphene.
146       The resulting composite featured a highly conductive multilayer graphene scaffold coated with
147   an approximately 200 nm thick layer of iron oxide. This material exhibited high electrochemical
148   capacity and reversibility, making it a strong candidate for conversion-type electrode materials. The
149   improved redox kinetics were attributed to the high electronic conductivity of graphene and the
150   intimate contact with the thin iron oxide layer, enhancing the overall electrochemical performance.

151   Suggestion: "Tensile strain" + "Molecular architecture"
152   Managing tensile strain has been underestimated in the design of stable interfaces for thin film
153   soft matter’ solar cells. In inorganic crystalline semiconductors, strain / stress management has
154   been understood as an important design criterion to prevent the formation of point defects, which
155   are triggered by both mechanical and thermal stress. However, in organic and perovskite solar
156   cells, the alternating combination of organic and inorganic interface materials are more and more
157   being established as a design criterion due to the advantage of orthogonal solubility in multi-
158   layer processing. Tensile strain then arises at the interface of materials with differing thermal
159   expansion coefficients during temperature fluctuations. Such fluctuations dominantly occur during
160   processing, annealing, and post-crystallization. The mismatch in thermal expansion coefficients
161   can cause macroscopic delamination or cracking at interfaces as well as microscopic formation of
162   point defects such as halide voids.
163       Furthermore, the molecular flexibility of interface materials, particularly their torsional flexi-
164   bility, significantly affects the mechanical properties of thin films, including the Young’s modulus.



                                                        24
165   Molecules with higher torsional flexibility can accommodate strain more effectively, enhancing the
166   mechanical resilience of the interface.
167       Recent research underscores that molecular tensile strain management might be regarded as
168   a more general proxy for interface design. Brabec and Friederich reported in Science 2024 [31]
169   torsional flexibility of HTL molecules, which was represented by the introduction of TPA units, as
170   one of several design criteria for efficient perovskite solar cells [31]. We note that the suggestion
171   "tensile strain + molecular architecture" was generated in December 2023. Properties such as
172   film formation and homogenous film quality benefited from that as well. In another currently
173   ongoing study by Brabec et al. (in submission), we found that embedding plasticizing molecules
174   dramatically increases the mechanical and operational stability of thin film solar cells.
175       In conclusion, the effective management of tensile strain through the selection of interface
176   materials with appropriate thermal expansion coefficients and molecular flexibility may have been
177   underestimated for the design of long-time stable thin film organic and hybrid solar cells.

178   Suggestion: "Multiphase structure" + "Selective laser melting"
179   The term microstructure refers to the complete set of internal structural features of a material.
180   This includes the crystallographic arrangement of its ideal, defect-free regions, as well as all types
181   of defects that may be present. These defects range from point defects - such as impurity atoms, va-
182   cancies, and interstitials - to line defects like dislocations, planar defects such as grain boundaries,
183   and three-dimensional defects including pores or precipitates. A well-characterized microstructure,
184   from the atomic to the macroscopic scale, enables the establishment of correlations between struc-
185   tural imperfections and the mechanical or functional properties of materials. This understanding
186   is essential for guiding the selection and application of materials in engineering contexts.
187       One important microstructural feature in complex metallic alloys and ceramic materials is
188   the presence of phases - regions within the material that exhibit uniform physical and chemical
189   properties, such as crystal structure and composition. Most technical materials are multiphase
190   systems, meaning they contain multiple such regions. The formation of these multiphase struc-
191   tures typically results from processing steps that involve heating and cooling cycles, and often
192   plastic deformation. These steps aim to achieve thermodynamically stable or kinetically stabilized
193   microstructures. Controlling the multiphase structure is critical, as it allows materials to exhibit a
194   desirable combination of properties, including enhanced strength, toughness, corrosion resistance,
195   wear resistance, and optimized electrical or magnetic behavior.
196       Selective Laser Melting (SLM) is an advanced additive manufacturing (3D printing) technique
197   in which a high-powered laser selectively melts and fuses fine layers of metal powder to fabricate
198   solid, complex parts directly from a digital design. As the part is built layer by layer from metal
199   powder, the process minimizes material waste while producing components with high detail and
200   mechanical integrity.
201       Due to the rapid heating and cooling cycles inherent in SLM, the resulting microstructures are
202   often far from equilibrium, frequently leading to the formation of multiphase materials. For exam-
203   ple, parts produced via SLM may contain mixtures of phases with differing crystal structures or
204   local compositions that would not typically form in slower, conventional manufacturing processes.
205   These multiphase structures can significantly affect the material’s final properties - potentially en-
206   hancing strength, hardness, or corrosion resistance, but also possibly introducing internal residual
207   stresses that could compromise long-term performance.

208   Suggestion: "Stress induced phase transformation" + "Hexagonal boron nitride"
209   The link between stress-induced phase transformation and hexagonal boron nitride (h-BN) is best
210   explained in the context of enhancing the fracture toughness of ceramics by phase transformations.
211   For instance, in zirconia, a tetragonal-to-monoclinic phase transformation results in a volume
212   expansion in the vicinity of a crack, which locally results in compressive stress both, deflecting
213   and stopping crack growth. This concept is applied to other metastable phases in hard coatings
214   [49]. A second concept of reaching an enormous tensile strength with simultaneously high fracture
215   toughness, which both typically exclude each other, is using local anisotropy. This was shown
216   in pearlite wires, where multiple micro-cracks parallel to the loading direction were formed and
217   enhanced the fracture toughness substantially [50]. There are at least two arguments why thinking
218   of stress-induced phase transformations and h-BN is reasonable. (i) Cubic boron nitride (c-BN),
219   having a Wurtzite crystal structure, is used as a hard coating because of its enormous hardness.


                                                        25
220   In contrast, the hardness of h-BN is significantly lower, rendering it not a top candidate for hard
221   coatings. On the contrary, h-BN offers a significant anisotropy in its mechanical properties and
222   therefore might locally help to produce micro-cracks parallel to the loading direction, resulting in
223   toughening. (ii) c-BN and h-BN have different densities, with c-BN exhibiting the higher one. I.e.,
224   upon compression, a certain propensity of forming c-BN exists. However, this phase transformation
225   seems not to be trivial, and it remains unclear if the c-BN to h-BN phase transformation could be
226   exploited from a materials engineering viewpoint. While first indications of improved mechanical
227   properties by using a c-BN / h-BN composite do exist [51], the role of phase transformations to
228   the best of our knowledge remains unknown for this system and could be an interesting topic for
229   future evaluation.

230   Suggestion: "In-plane polarization" + "Organic solar cell"
231   A number of material properties were discovered in perovskite photovoltaics that have not been
232   considered with respect to photovoltaics in the past, but which may benefit the generation and
233   extraction of photogenerated charge carriers and hence the power conversion efficiency of the
234   devices. Among these properties, a ferroelectric in-plane polarization of methylammonium lead
235   iodide (MAPbI3) was vividly discussed to help with the separation of electrons and holes as well as
236   their conduction to the respective electrodes along ferroelectric domain walls [32,33]. So far, the
237   ferroelectric properties are genuine to MAPbI3 solar cells and some derivatives thereof [35]. The
238   necessary precondition is a non-centrosymmetric unit cell of the crystal, which is why this concept
239   has not played a role in any of the established solar technologies, such as silicon, so far [35,36].
240   Yet, it may well play a role in future novel perovskite-inspired materials [52].
241       The requirement of crystallinity and non-centrosymmetric unit cells seemingly does not link
242   to organic solar cells, which are mostly amorphous. Yet, the great design flexibility of organic
243   molecules can be used to introduce asymmetric polar functional groups, which are already in use
244   today in piezoelectric polymers for organic touch sensors, e.g., polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF). If,
245   in addition, ways are found to orient these molecules, an organic analogy to in-plane polarization
246   may be designed, which then also benefits the charge carrier separation in organic solar cells. The
247   challenge here is that in-plane polarization is notoriously difficult to control via external electrical
248   poling since the electrodes in organic solar cells only allow for the application of an out-of-plane
249   electric field [37]. Hence, in-plane polarization probably must be part of the conformation of the
250   film itself through post-processing or by molecular self-assembly. Covalent organic frameworks
251   (COFs) may also mimic a crystal structure, which then can be modified for in-plane polarization.
252       Furthermore, polarizable molecules typically have higher dielectric constants, which reduce the
253   binding energy of photo-generated electron-hole pairs. While this binding energy still limits the
254   performance of organic solar cells today, increasing the dielectric constant through polarization may
255   help to enhance the open-circuit voltage of solar cells and to reduce monomolecular recombination.
256   The idea of increasing the dielectric constant of organic molecules to reduce the binding energy of
257   electron-hole pairs has been around for many years, yet without any major breakthroughs. The
258   use of polarizing functional groups has not been thoroughly investigated so far.
259       As of today, both resulting concepts are rather speculative, but the machine learning suggestion
260   points in an interesting research direction to be considered in future experiments.

281   8     LLM Fine-tuning
282   Hugging Face’s Python library was used to fine-tune the LLaMa-2-13B model [41,53]. We prepare
283   our annotated data by constructing a single string per data point as follows <s> + abstract +
284   "#KEYWORDS#" + concepts + </s> where concepts corresponds to the string version of a Python
285   list, e.g. [’brittle epoxy’, ’butadiene nitrile liquid rubber’, ...]. <s> and </s> rep-
286   resent the beginning of sequence (BOS) and, respectively, end of sequence (EOS) token for the
287   LLaMa models. #KEYWORDS# serves as an own custom delimiter to signal the model when concept
288   extraction should start. For the LLaMa tokenizer, we set pad_token_id = 0, bos_token_id =
289   1, and eos_token_id = 2. Being a CausalLM, we set the labels equal to the input token ids.
290   Our LoraConfig is r = 16 (Lora attention dimension), lora_alpha = 32, target_modules=["
291   q_proj", "v_proj"] (modules where the adapter is applied), and lora_dropout = 0.01 (dropout
292   probability) [44][dettmers2023qlora]. Training took around an hour on a single A100 GPU.
